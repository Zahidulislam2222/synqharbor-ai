# Legal readiness and responsible AI use

This guide identifies applicability and implementation work for an operator; it does not replace legal advice or certify a deployment. Determine the business/operator role, markets, user ages/locations, data categories, sector, vendors, regions and intended AI use before deciding which obligations apply.

## EU/EEA assessment

Where GDPR applies, document controller/processor roles, purpose and lawful basis, notices, minimization, privacy by design, security, rights handling, retention, processors/DPAs, international transfers and incident obligations. Assess whether a DPIA is required for likely high-risk processing; the presence of AI alone is not a complete applicability test.

Assess EU AI Act provider/deployer role and intended use. General drafting/coding must not silently become employment, credit, health or another high-impact decision system. Check prohibited practices, relevant transparency/AI-literacy duties and any high-risk obligations using current official guidance. Retain human authority, provenance and intended-use limitations. Model openness does not automatically remove every operator obligation.

## United States assessment

Assess FTC consumer-protection/privacy duties, applicable state privacy and breach laws, children's-data requirements where relevant, contractual commitments and sector-specific obligations. HIPAA applies to the relevant covered-entity/business-associate processing, not every software project. Before ePHI, establish service eligibility, BAAs, vendor/subprocessor coverage, access/security, incident and recovery controls. Consumer health information may trigger requirements beyond HIPAA depending on the actual service.

## Contracts, intellectual property and accessibility

Public source is Apache-2.0; dependency, font, artwork and model licenses retain their own terms. Users must have rights to source documents/code and permission for repository changes. AI-generated output requires human verification for correctness, rights and suitability. Customer agreements must define service scope, privacy/processing terms, support, availability commitments, liability and exit/export expectations. Assess applicable accessibility and consumer rules for the actual market.

## Release evidence and remaining work

Implemented technical controls are described in [security](security.md). Deployment-specific contacts, processing location, contracts, age/sector policy, rights operations, retention enforcement, administrator MFA, infrastructure encryption qualification and legal review remain separate acceptance gates before sensitive or regulated customer use. Review this assessment when purposes, features, regions, vendors or laws change.

Official sources: [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme_en), [European Commission AI Act guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act), [FTC data protection](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business), [HHS cloud guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html).
